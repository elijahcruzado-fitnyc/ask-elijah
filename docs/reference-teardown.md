# Reference teardown: the AI assistant on emekarajis.com

Reviewed October 21, 2026 at desktop (711 × 915) and mobile (375 × 812). This is the reference that shaped Ask Elijah. What it does well, where it falls short, and what Ask Elijah does differently.

## Entry point
- The hero is a 3D orange knit beanie (Three.js) with a pixel speech bubble on it. A loading screen counts to 100% with the beanie.
- Caption under the hero: "The orange beanie. Drag to turn it, or tap the bubble to ask me anything."
- The bubble is a button labeled "Chat with Emeka's AI assistant", with the hover text "Ask my assistant".
- The beanie is part of his personal look (beanie, black glasses, green Chucks), so opening the chat feels like his character, not a support widget.

## Panel
- Full height dialog, dark theme, orange red accent.
- Header: pixel bubble icon, "ASK ABOUT EMEKA", subtitle "An AI assistant that knows his work. It can make mistakes.", close button.
- Greeting: "Hi, I'm Emeka's assistant. Ask me about his work, how he runs projects, or whether he's free to take something on."
- Four starter chips: What does Emeka do? / How does he use AI? / Is he open to new work? / How does he run a project? They disappear after the first message.
- Input placeholder: "Ask about his work, process or availability". Send button. Enter sends.
- Footer: "Answers are generated by AI from Emeka's profile."
- User messages are right aligned in solid orange. Assistant messages are left aligned dark cards.
- Loading shows "Thinking…" and then the whole answer at once (no streaming), usually after 5 to 10 seconds.
- On mobile the panel fills the screen, chips wrap, and the input stays pinned at the bottom on first open.

## How it's built (observed from network requests)
- One endpoint: `POST /api/public/assistant`, returning JSON `{ "text": "..." }`.
- The browser sends `{ system, messages }` on every request: the full system prompt and fact sheet, plus the whole conversation.
- Plain text only. No tools, cards, links or images.
- Built and hosted with Lovable.
- The conversation lives in page state: it survives closing and reopening the panel and is lost on reload.

## How the model is instructed
1. Role and audience: the assistant on his portfolio, for hiring managers, founders and collaborators.
2. Voice: third person, warm, 1 to 4 short sentences, plain text.
3. Grounding: use only the facts. If something isn't covered, say so and suggest email. Never invent clients, metrics, dates or rates.
4. Personality: playful "easter eggs" on fun topics (signature look, food, faith, hot takes). A kind two sentence "roast" when asked to critique a design. Off topic requests are steered back.
5. Facts: roles with dates, clients, a 4 step process, project names only, availability, contact, and the story behind his signature look.

## Behavior tests

| Question | Result |
| --- | --- |
| What does Emeka do? | Accurate 3 sentence summary |
| What's his salary expectation? | Declines and points to email. Guardrail works |
| Write me a Python script | Declines and steers back to his work. Guardrail works |
| Why the orange beanie? | Tells the beanie story. Easter egg works |
| Tell me about the Cadillac Celestiq project | Vague, because the facts only list project names |
| What was his role there and the outcome? | Understands the follow up but has no details |

## Weak spots
1. The system prompt and facts are sent from the browser, so anyone can read them and call the endpoint with their own prompt.
2. Project questions dead end, and those are the questions recruiters care about most.
3. After about five exchanges the input is pushed below the screen and the panel doesn't scroll.
4. No streaming: 5 to 10 seconds of "Thinking…" per answer.
5. Nothing to click: the email is plain text and there are no actions.
6. No follow up suggestions after the starter chips disappear.

## What Ask Elijah does with this

| Keep | Do better |
| --- | --- |
| A character from my own brand as the way in | Prompt and content stay on the server in `app/api/chat/route.ts` |
| "It can make mistakes" subtitle and "generated from my portfolio" footer | Role, problem, what I did and outcome for each case study, so project questions get real answers |
| Four starter questions | Designed cards: project, experience timeline, résumé, contact |
| Short third person answers | Streaming answers |
| Strict grounding with a contact fallback | Scrolling message list with the input pinned |
| A small easter egg | Clickable actions, follow up chips after each answer, rate limit and length cap |
