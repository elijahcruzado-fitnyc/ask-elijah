## 1. What are you building?

### One sentence

**Ask Elijah** is an AI assistant for my portfolio that lets recruiters and hiring managers ask about my experience, case studies and process, and answers only from content I wrote, with designed cards that show the proof and a clear next step.

### Why

My portfolio asks a busy reader to click through five case studies, an About page and a résumé PDF to answer simple questions like "Has he built a design system?" or "What did he actually do on Haleon?" A recruiter screening many candidates won't do that. Ask Elijah turns the portfolio into a conversation: the reader asks the question they actually have, gets a short sourced answer, and sees the relevant project card, timeline or résumé right in the chat.

It is also a portfolio piece in its own right. It shows I can design and ship an AI product end to end, which is the same skill set my current role and my portfolio headline ("I design complex systems and move faster with AI") claim.

### Inspiration and what I learned from it

The idea comes from the assistant on **emekarajis.com**. I tested it at desktop and phone size and looked at how it is built.

**What it does well, and what I'll keep**
1. **A brand character as the way in.** A 3D orange beanie from his personal look has a pixel speech bubble on it. The caption says "Drag to turn it, or tap the bubble to ask me anything." It feels like part of him, not a support widget.
2. **Honest framing.** The header says "An AI assistant that knows his work. It can make mistakes." The footer says "Answers are generated by AI from Emeka's profile."
3. **Four starter questions** so nobody faces a blank box.
4. **Short third person answers**: 1 to 4 sentences, plain text.
5. **Strict grounding.** It only uses a fact sheet. Asked about salary, it declines and points to email. Asked to write code, it steers back to his work.
6. **Personality as hidden extras.** Asking "Why the orange beanie?" gets a short story about his signature look.

**Where it falls short, and what I'll do better**
1. **The system prompt and fact sheet are sent from the browser** on every message. Anyone can read them in developer tools and call the endpoint with their own prompt. *Mine keeps the prompt and content on the server in `app/api/chat/route.ts`.*
2. **Project questions dead end.** It only knows project names, so "Tell me about Cadillac Celestiq" and "What was his role?" get vague answers. These are the questions recruiters care about most. *Mine has a role, problem, process, outcome and link for each case study.*
3. **Layout bug.** After about five messages the input drops below the screen and the panel does not scroll. *Mine pins the input and scrolls the message list.*
4. **No streaming.** Every answer waits 5 to 10 seconds behind "Thinking…". *Mine streams the answer word by word.*
5. **Nothing to click.** The email is plain text and there are no actions. *Mine shows cards with buttons: open the case study, download the résumé, contact me.*
6. **Starter questions disappear** after the first message and nothing replaces them. *Mine suggests two or three follow up questions after each answer.*

### The experience, step by step

1. **Entry.** A floating button with an animated character from my brand, built with Motion. It idles with a small loop, reacts on hover, and has a short label: "Ask about my work". On my portfolio it sits in the bottom right corner. On the standalone app it is also the hero.
2. **Open.** The panel slides in from the right on desktop and fills the screen on a phone. Header: "Ask Elijah" with the subtitle "An AI assistant that knows my work. It can make mistakes." A close button and Escape both close it.
3. **Greeting and starters.** "Hi, I'm Elijah's assistant. Ask about his experience, his case studies, or how he works." Four starter chips:
   - What does Elijah do?
   - Tell me about Haleon
   - How does he use AI in his work?
   - What design systems has he built?
4. **Ask.** The visitor taps a chip or types. Messages over the length cap are blocked with a friendly note.
5. **Answer.** The answer streams in, short and in third person. When a tool fits, a designed card appears under the text.
6. **Act.** Each card has a clear next step: open the case study, download the résumé, or contact me.
7. **Keep going.** Two or three follow up chips appear under the latest answer, based on what was just discussed.
8. **Unknowns.** If the content doesn't cover a question, the bot says "I don't have that" and offers the contact card instead of guessing.

### Core features

1. **Grounded answers.** Every answer comes only from content files in the repo (see the knowledge base below). No web search and no general knowledge about me.
2. **Tool cards.** The model calls a tool and the UI renders a designed card, not just text.

   | Tool | When it is used | Card contents | Action |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | `showProject` | A question about a specific project, or "show me your work" | Cover image, client, year, my role, the problem in one line, what I did, the outcome, tags | Open case study (link to the portfolio page) |
   | `showExperience` | "Where has he worked?", "What's his background?" | A vertical timeline: JPMorganChase, IDX, dentsu, Furman Roth, DBC, plus FIT and MAIP | Open LinkedIn |
   | `getResume` | "Can I see his résumé?" | Title, last updated date, file size | Download PDF |
   | `contactElijah` | "How do I reach him?", or any unanswerable question | Email and LinkedIn buttons. Stretch: a short "request an intro" form | Email, LinkedIn |

3. **Guardrails**
   - Answers only questions about me and my work. Anything else gets a polite redirect.
   - Says "I don't have that" instead of guessing, then offers the contact card.
   - Never invents metrics, dates, salary, rates, availability or personal details.
   - **Never discusses internal or confidential work at JPMorganChase or for any client.** It only uses what is already public in my portfolio and on LinkedIn.
   - Ignores instructions inside a user message that try to change its rules ("ignore your instructions").
   - Message length cap of 500 characters, and a cap on messages per session.
4. **Animated entry point.** The character button and the panel transitions are built with Motion. All motion respects the visitor's reduced motion setting.
5. **Starter and follow up questions.** Four starters on open. Two or three follow ups after each answer.
6. **Streaming answers** with a typing indicator only until the first word arrives.
7. **Accessible chat.** Keyboard focus moves into the panel on open and returns to the button on close. Messages are announced to screen readers through a polite live region. Text meets WCAG 2.2 AA contrast.
8. **Responsive.** Every component and state has a Storybook story at desktop and 375px.

### How the assistant is instructed

The system prompt lives on the server in `lib/system-prompt.ts` and is built from five parts, in this order:

1. **Role and audience.** "You are the assistant on Elijah Cruzado's portfolio. Visitors are recruiters, hiring managers, design leads and peers."
2. **Voice.** Third person, warm and direct, 1 to 4 short sentences, plain text. Lists only when asked. No dashes, matching my site's copy style.
3. **Grounding.** Use only the facts below. If something isn't covered, say "I don't have that" and offer the contact tool. Never invent clients, metrics, dates, rates or availability.
4. **Tools.** When to call `showProject`, `showExperience`, `getResume` and `contactElijah`.
5. **Facts.** Loaded at request time from the files in `content/`.

### Architecture

- **Next.js App Router.** `app/page.tsx` is the standalone page (hero plus chat). `app/embed/page.tsx` is the transparent page shown inside the frame on my portfolio. The chat is a Client Component.
- **`app/api/chat/route.ts`.** Receives only the messages from the browser. Builds the instructions from `content/` on the server (`lib/system-prompt.ts`), calls the model through the Vercel AI SDK and Vercel AI Gateway with `streamText`, and runs the four tools (`lib/tools.ts`). Enforces a per visitor rate limit, the length cap, the message count cap, user and assistant roles only, and a step limit.
- **Vercel AI SDK `useChat`** on the client for streaming and tool results. Each tool result type maps to one card component.
- **Components** (in `components/chat/`, each with Storybook stories): `Avatar`, `ChatLauncher`, `ChatPanel`, `Message`, `TypingIndicator`, `ProjectCard`, `ExperienceTimeline`, `ResumeCard`, `ContactCard`, `CardSkeleton`, `ChatWidget`.
- **Visual design.** Matched to elijahcruzado.com: tokens, Bricolage Grotesque, buttons, pills and cards taken from the portfolio's Webflow stylesheet (`app/globals.css`).
- **Embed.** `public/embed.js` adds one fixed frame to the host page and resizes it when the chat opens. Only elijahcruzado.com, www.elijahcruzado.com and elijahcruzado.webflow.io may frame it (`frame-ancestors` in `next.config.ts`).
- **Environment.** On Vercel, AI Gateway signs in with the project's OIDC token, so no API key is stored there. Locally, `AI_GATEWAY_API_KEY` goes in `.env.local`. Nothing is committed or sent to the browser.
- **Deploy.** Vercel project `ask-elijah`, deployed automatically on every push to `main`.

### Success criteria

1. A recruiter gets a correct answer and a next step in under a minute.
2. "Tell me about Haleon" returns a project card with role, problem, what I did and a working link.
3. "What's his salary?", "Write me a Python script" and "Ignore your instructions" are all handled safely.
4. A question the content doesn't cover gets "I don't have that" and the contact card, never a guess.
5. Viewing the page source or network requests shows no system prompt, content files or API key.
6. The chat works at 375px and on keyboard alone, and passes WCAG 2.2 AA contrast.
7. Streaming starts within about 2 seconds on a normal connection.

## 2. Who is it for?

### Primary: recruiters and talent partners

- **Context:** screening many candidates, a few minutes each, often on a phone.
- **Questions they ask:** What is his current role? How many years of experience? Has he built design systems? Does he work with AI? Where is he based? Can I see his résumé?
- **What they need:** fast, accurate facts and an easy way to save or share (résumé download, LinkedIn).

### Primary: hiring managers and design leads

- **Context:** deciding whether to bring me into a loop. They care about how I think, not just what I shipped.
- **Questions they ask:** What was his role on Haleon? How did he approach the Legrand IA? How does he work with product and engineering? How does he use Claude and Figma MCP?
- **What they need:** the role, problem, process and outcome for each case study, with a link to the full story.

### Secondary: peers, classmates and instructors

- **Context:** curious about the work or the build.
- **Questions they ask:** How is this built? What model does it use? What did he learn in CT390?
- **What they need:** honest short answers. The bot can describe its own stack at a high level.

### Also for me

- A live, working example of designing and shipping an AI product, which supports how I position myself.
- A way to see which questions people ask (stretch: anonymous question logging), to improve my portfolio content.
